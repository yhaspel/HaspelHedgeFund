# Sector / Thematic ETF Rotation

**The goal of sector rotation is to bet on *which parts of the market are in favour* rather than on individual stocks — by holding the strongest sector and theme ETFs and rotating as leadership changes.** Use it when you think in top-down terms ("technology is leading, energy is lagging") and want to express that view simply, without picking single companies. It suits the investor who follows market themes and wants a tactical, regime-aware way to tilt toward strength.

## How it works

Instead of stocks, the strategy holds a handful of **ETFs** — each one a ready-made basket covering a sector or theme (technology, banks, energy, semiconductors, gold miners, biotech, and so on). It is long-only by default.

Each cycle the strategy **ranks** the available ETFs by recent strength (momentum), how deep their recent drawdowns are, and how well they fit the current macro **regime** — then concentrates the book in the top few. As leadership rotates, the holdings rotate with it. Overlapping ETFs (say, a broad technology fund and a semiconductor fund) are de-duplicated so you do not unknowingly double up on the same theme.

## What the AI council does

The debate happens at the **sector level** rather than the single-stock level — the council assesses themes, not companies. It weighs whether a sector's recent strength is durable or stretched, and whether the macro backdrop supports it, before the Portfolio Manager commits to the top-ranked ETFs.

## Key settings

- **ETF universe** — the set of sector and theme ETFs to rotate among.
- **Number of holdings** — how many top-ranked ETFs to hold (typically 3–6).
- **Regime sensitivity** — how much the macro regime influences the ranking.
- **Cost ceiling per cycle** — the hard cap on AI spend.

## Best for / not ideal for

**Best for:** investors who think top-down in themes and sectors; those who want tactical tilts without single-stock research.

**Not ideal for:** users who want stock-specific alpha (use a stock-picking strategy), or who want a hands-off, low-turnover book (rotation trades as leadership shifts).

## What to watch

Rotation can lag at sharp turning points — it tends to chase strength, so it can buy a theme just as it peaks or sell just as it bottoms. Watch turnover: every rotation costs trading and council spend, and frequent flip-flopping erodes returns.
