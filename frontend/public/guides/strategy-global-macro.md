# Global Macro (ETF Expression)

**The goal of a global macro strategy is to take a top-down view of the whole economy — growth, inflation, interest rates, the dollar — and express it through broad ETFs.** Use it when you form opinions about the big picture ("growth is slowing, so bonds should do well") and want to act on them without trading futures or currencies directly. It is the strategy for the macro thinker who wants a simple, ETF-only way to put a worldview into a portfolio.

## How it works

The macro agent classifies the current **regime** — the prevailing growth, inflation, and policy environment — and the strategy buys the broad **ETFs** that historically suit that regime. For example: long-duration bond ETFs when growth is slowing, gold when inflation is sticky, broad equity ETFs when expansion is underway.

To bet *against* a market, it buys an **inverse ETF** — a fund engineered to rise when its index falls — rather than short-selling. That means the strategy never has to borrow shares or pay borrow fees; every position is a straightforward purchase.

## What the AI council does

The **macro agent is the lead voice** here. The council's debate is about the state of the world and which asset classes fit it, not about individual companies. The Portfolio Manager translates the agreed macro view into a concrete set of ETF holdings.

## Key settings

- **ETF universe** — the broad asset-class and inverse ETFs available to express macro views.
- **Regime sensitivity** — how strongly the macro regime drives allocation.
- **Regime gate** — an optional trigger that shifts the book defensive when a bear regime becomes likely.
- **Cost ceiling per cycle** — the hard cap on AI spend.

## Best for / not ideal for

**Best for:** investors who follow the economy and central banks and want to act on that lens; those who want macro exposure without futures or FX accounts.

**Not ideal for:** users who want company-specific, bottom-up stock picking — global macro is the opposite approach.

## What to watch

Macro calls are hard and can be wrong for a long time before they pay off, or never. Regime classification lags slightly at turning points. Inverse ETFs are designed for short holding periods and can drift from a simple "opposite of the index" over long stretches — keep an eye on how long inverse positions are held.
